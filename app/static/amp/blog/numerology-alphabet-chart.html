What Is a Numerology Alphabet Chart?

In numerology, an alphabet chart is a master conversion grid that assigns specific single-digit numerical values to each letter of the alphabet. When names or words are evaluated, each letter is converted to its corresponding number, and the digits are summed to yield single-digit root vibrations and compound numbers.

Two dominant traditions exist in global numerological practice: the Chaldean sound frequency system (originating in ancient Babylon) and the Pythagorean sequential system (formalized in the classical Greek philosophical tradition). Understanding how each system maps letters is fundamental to accurate analysis.

Why Is Number 9 Omitted from Chaldean Single Letters?

The Sacred Crown of Number 9

In ancient Chaldean cosmology, the number 9 was regarded as the highest, sacred vibration representing perfection, spiritual culmination, and the unmanifested divine spark. As a result, no single letter in the alphabet was directly attributed the number 9. However, number 9 can naturally appear as the compound sum of a full name (for example, compound 18, 27, 36, or 45), indicating a destiny of universal service and completion.

In contrast, the Pythagorean system assigns numbers 1 through 9 strictly sequentially based on alphabetical position (A=1, B=2 ... I=9, J=1 ... R=9, S=1 ... Z=8).

Worked Example: Calculating a Name

Step-by-Step Calculation: "AARAV SHARMA"

Chaldean Method:

  • A-A-R-A-V: 1 + 1 + 2 + 1 + 6 = 11 (Master Number / Compound 11)
  • S-H-A-R-M-A: 3 + 5 + 1 + 2 + 4 + 1 = 16 (Compound 16)
  • Total Compound: 11 + 16 = 27
  • Root Number: 2 + 7 = 9 (Mars — Courage, Humanitarian Leadership)

Pythagorean Method:

  • A-A-R-A-V: 1 + 1 + 9 + 1 + 4 = 16 -> 1 + 6 = 7
  • S-H-A-R-M-A: 1 + 8 + 1 + 9 + 4 + 1 = 24 -> 2 + 4 = 6
  • Total Expression: 7 + 6 = 13 -> 1 + 3 = 4 (Practical Structure)

Notice how the two systems arrive at different root archetypes. This occurs because Chaldean examines spoken phonetic resonance, whereas Pythagorean evaluates sequential geometry.

Best Practices: Avoiding System Mixing

A common mistake in amateur numerology is combining Chaldean letter values with Pythagorean compound interpretations, or calculating the first name using Chaldean and the surname using Pythagorean. Doing so invalidates the mathematical coherence of both traditions.
